Welcome

Learn to read between the headlines

Every day, headlines and posts compete for a few seconds of your attention. Some are honest. Some stretch the truth. Some are built entirely to trigger a feeling before you've read a single fact.

This lab won't quiz you on media literacy facts. Instead, you'll look at real-style examples, form your own reaction first, and then we'll reveal what's actually happening underneath — so the lesson sticks.

Two short challenges. No prior knowledge needed. Just your instincts.
Concept · Headline Accuracy

Challenge 1: Headline Detective

Headlines are written to make you click — that's their job. But there's a difference between an interesting headline and a misleading one. Misleading headlines exaggerate, omit context, or imply something the article doesn't actually support.

Why it matters: if you only ever read headlines (most people do), a misleading one can plant a false belief in seconds — and that belief often survives even after you learn the truth.

Concept · Emotional Manipulation

Challenge 2: Emotion Detector

Some content isn't trying to inform you at all — it's trying to make you feel something fast: outrage, fear, envy, urgency. That feeling makes you share, comment, or buy before you've thought it through.

Why it matters: content that triggers strong emotion spreads faster than calm, accurate content — which is exactly why so much of what goes viral is engineered to provoke, not inform.
